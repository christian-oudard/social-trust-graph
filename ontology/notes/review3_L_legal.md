## A. Wrong or doctrinally contrary (fix first)

### A1. "Out-of-scope promises bind nobody: the registry makes every scope public, so there is no apparent authority and no warranty of authority" (core.lp:190-191; README L2; v0.3 "reliance ... is never reasonable") — **wrong as a statement of law; defensible only as a stipulation**
- Warranty of authority (R3d Agency §6.10) is excluded only if the third party *knows* the actor lacks authority (or the actor disclaims, or the principal ratifies). Constructive knowledge from a register is not one of the exceptions. Source: https://garrettham.com/breach-of-warranty-of-authority/ (reproducing §6.10); survey_D §2.1.
- Apparent authority (§2.03) turns on the third party's reasonable belief traceable to the principal's manifestations. Placing an agent in a role/office is itself such a manifestation (§3.03), and it lingers after revocation (§3.11). The README's own Limits concede the registry-lag gap.
- Positive law mostly **rejects** "public filing = notice of limits on authority":
  - UK Companies Act 2006 s.40(1): directors' power "is deemed to be free of any limitation under the company's constitution"; s.40(2)(b)(i): a person dealing "is not bound to enquire". https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/40
  - Directive (EU) 2017/1132 Art 9(2): limits on organs' powers "may not be relied on as against third parties, even if they have been disclosed." https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2017/1132/article/9
  - RUPA (1997) §303(f): a non-partner "is not deemed to know of a limitation on the authority of a partner merely because the limitation is contained in a filed statement"; the only exception is recorded limits on transferring real property (§303(e)). https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/2016/title-7/partnerships/article-64/part-3/section-7-64-303/
  - Constructive notice by filing does exist, but in narrow places: real-property recording, UCC Art. 9 "notice filing" (which concerns priority, not authority), and RUPA §704 statements of dissociation (deemed notice after 90 days).
- The legal footing that does support the design is **UNCITRAL MLAC Art 7(1)**: "As between the parties ... an action carried out by an automated system is attributed in accordance with a procedure agreed to by the parties." Without such an agreement, **Art 7(2)–(3)** attribute the action to "the person who uses the system", even where "the outcome was unexpected". That is the opposite of "binds nobody". Source: https://uncitral.un.org/sites/uncitral.un.org/files/mlac_en.pdf (A/79/17 Annex IV, verified).
- **Minimal fix (core.lp:190):** "Out-of-scope promises bind nobody. By stipulation, the registry is the only manifestation of authority and every party is deemed to have notice of it, as if the parties had agreed to it as their attribution procedure (cf. UNCITRAL MLAC Art 7(1)). Positive law usually refuses such deemed notice (CA 2006 s.40; Dir. 2017/1132 Art 9(2); RUPA §303(f)), so apparent authority and warranty of authority are excluded here by design, not by doctrine." In v0.3, change "never reasonable" to "treated as unreasonable by stipulation".

### A2. Vitiation: "an invocation steered by someone it does not act for (fraud, duress, prompt injection) cannot change the registry: its creations ... are void" (core.lp:91-93; v0.2 #16 "void") — **wrong term; stricter than law**
- R2d Contracts §164 and §175 make fraud and duress by threat **voidable**, and voidable at the victim's election. Only fraud in the factum (§163) and physical compulsion (§174) prevent assent, making the contract void.
- Third-party fraud or duress does not avoid the contract against a counterparty who gave value or relied materially in good faith (§164(2), §175(2)). The core voids even then.
- Source: https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/1st+Semester/LAW+505-002+%E2%80%93+Contracts+I/R2C+%C2%A7+164
- **Fix:** "...are treated as void (a deliberate simplification: law makes fraud and duress by threat voidable at the victim's option, R2d §§164, 175, and protects a good-faith counterparty against third-party fraud)."

### A3. Security termination: "a security ends once the breach it secures is repaired" (core.lp:102; README L3/L9) — **divergent from the cited-in-spirit doctrine; also a design gap**
- A power given as security (R3d Agency §3.12) survives revocation by its creator. That part is verified, and the core matches it: https://quizlet.com/788059400/restatement-third-of-agency-2006-flash-cards/ ; survey_D.
- It terminates when **the obligation secured is discharged** (§3.13; recalled, exact wording **unverified**), not when a first breach is cured.
- The rule `ended_by(G,T) :- security(G), trigger(G,C), repaired(C,_,T1)` ends the security after the first repaired breach. A second breach of an ongoing *avoid* commitment is then unsecured (compare x4(b), "repair is per breach").
- **Fix:** either re-arm the security per breach, or say "ends once the first secured breach is repaired (narrower than R3d §3.13, where a power given as security lasts until the secured obligation is discharged)". Also cite §3.12 by name. The doctrine is used but never cited.

## C. Accurate (verified)

- **R3d Agency §1.04 cmt e**, "tools" (README §7). The verbatim quote is "a computer program is not capable of acting as a principal or an agent as defined by the common law. At present, computer programs are instrumentalities of the persons who use them." Suggest keeping the "at present" hedge. https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/19-1-4-bayern-final_0.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.07913
- **R2d §318(3)** (survey): delegation does not discharge the delegating obligor. The core's "no change of content ends a commitment" is consistent.
- **R2d §317(2)**: not cited, but relevant. "Privileges are personal" inverts contract law's default for *rights* (assignable unless a material change to the obligor). It matches licensing (*In re CFLC*), so it is fine as a design choice. Say so if L3 is read as law.
- **R3d §5.03** (uncited): "imputed knowledge sticks" and "consent imputes nothing" are consistent. Note that §5.03 imputes only facts *material to the agent's duties*, whereas L7 imputes everything (conservative, but not the law).
- **R3d §3.15** (uncited): sub-delegation needs authority, and the appointing agent remains responsible. Consistent with L6/s4.
- **UNCITRAL MLAC (2024)**: survey quotes of Art 7, bracketed Art 8 and Art 10 were checked against A/79/17 Annex IV and are accurate. The deliverable does not cite MLAC; see A1 for where it should.
- **GDPR Art 28(2)**: the survey quote is accurate. Application: see B3.
- **EU AI Act Art 43(4)** (survey, uncited; the S2(iii) "change envelope"): the predetermined-change carve-out applies to systems "that continue to learn" and to changes documented per Annex IV 2(f). The survey quote omits the "not foreseen or planned in the initial conformity assessment" qualifier in the definition. https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/43/
- **Governatori ⊗ reparation chains**: accurate, including a reparation debtor different from the primary debtor (`bond`). survey_A §4 (EDOC 2006).
- **Symboleo liable/performer/rightHolder**: accurate (ER 2020 axioms 1-4; survey_A §6.1).
- **Macaroon/Biscuit/UCAN attenuation** (min caps, conjunctive caveats, revocation of derivatives): accurate. UCAN: "Each direct delegation MUST either directly restate or attenuate"; "Revocation is irreversible ... all of its derivatives ... MUST be marked as invalid." https://github.com/ucan-wg/spec
- **Event-calculus inertia**: accurate. `live` is the closed-form HoldsAt ← Initiates ∧ ¬Clipped.
- **Alloy "recommended unless defaults proved central"**: accurate paraphrase of survey_B ("If defeasibility turns out to be central ... switch the spec of record to clingo plus event calculus"). Nit: the survey's trigger also named "priorities among norms", which the core does not use.
- **"TLA+ lacks defaults"**: accurate. It is classical, with frames via UNCHANGED.
- **s6 "cf. published commitments to preserve retired models' weights"**: accurate as a "cf.". Anthropic's commitment is unilateral and public, not addressed *to* a model. https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments

## D. Other overclaiming or loaded wording
- "Evidence is a promise someone can be held to" / "References are warranties": in law, references give rise to negligent-misstatement or defamation exposure, not warranty. Say "modelled as warranties".
- "Warranty of authority removed ... replaces 3 rules" (v0.3): frame it as a scope reduction, not as a doctrinal consequence (A1).
